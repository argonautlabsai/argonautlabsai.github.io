A 2.8-trillion-parameter model on one laptop, streamed from SSDs — and the measurements of why it runs at the speed it does.

ARGODRIVE is the storage layer and the instruments we built to run Kimi K3 (93 layers, 896 experts per layer, 1.45 TB of expert weights) on a single MacBook Pro with 128 GB, reading the experts from four NVMe drives at decode time. Everything below is one cold run with the exact prompt, with the logs published.

What the measurements say

A mixture-of-experts layer needs 16 expert reads and cannot proceed until the slowest one lands. So a layer costs the maximum over its reads, not the sum: total bandwidth converts poorly into speed, and per-read latency converts well. Four drives at 90–100% of their individual ceilings still leave the barrier in charge.

What we are careful to say

  • Output is token-identical between drafter on and off on a given storage layout; not claimed across layouts.
  • Expert weights are untouched at their released MXFP4 precision; the resident trunk is int8, which upstream labels non-weight-exact.
  • 1 tok/s is not for chat. It is for unattended work — scheduled review and reporting on data that must stay on the machine.
  • All measurements are from one machine. Small effects on this rig need replication before they are believed.
