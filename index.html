DEEPSEEK V4.1 FLASH Q4 / DS4

Prefill was reading one drive.
And twice the bytes.

A 518 GB model streamed from NVMe on a 128 GB laptop. Prefill went through the memory map of the primary file, so one drive moved 315.9 GB while the others sat idle — and the layer-major sweep read all 384 experts per layer where the prompt routes to 187. Staging every layer through the weighted split read, then reading only the selected experts. Measured against the pinned upstream binary with a byte-identical output hash on every arm.

The two upstream figures are the controls for the two experiments, each measured in its own interleaved session: the first applies to the one-drive column, the second to the three-drive column. The three-drive decode figure includes the GPU keep-alive added on 17 September (four interleaved pairs, output identical); before it the same column measured 17.38. Control is the pinned upstream ds4 binary bd66c402; every arm produced the same output SHA-256, so the comparison is like-for-like. The one-drive column needs no replicas at all — it is the selective-expert read alone, and it is the portable part of this result. The three-drive column adds weighted split reads across internal + two SN8100s at 10:5:5, which is what the published branch supports. Replicas are byte-identical copies of the 518 GB file. On a separate 512/512 rung the fork also measured 22.4% faster than upstream on a single internal SSD before any enclosure was added — see the 14 September matched report.

05 / ARGONAUT LABS

Independent research.
Work you can inspect.

We investigate storage, memory and scheduling for large-model inference on local hardware. Argodrive grew out of these experiments: a way to see the bottlenecks, compare changes and keep the evidence.

Our work builds on Salvatore Sanfilippo’s ds4 and gavamedia’s deltafin, and the wider llama.cpp and GGML ecosystem. Development and review use Claude, ChatGPT and OpenAI Codex, with human direction and measured validation.
